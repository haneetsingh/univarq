# Univarq — Copy Review v2 (less "architecture," more breadth + full-solution framing)

## What changed from v1

The last pass leaned hard on "architecture" as the core differentiator. That's accurate but narrow — it undersells that Univarq covers a wide technology surface (frontend, backend, cloud, data, AI) and delivers finished, working systems, not just design documents. This pass pulls "architecture" back to one capability among several, and pushes "we build the whole thing, end to end" as the throughline instead.

---

## 1. Hero Section

**Before:**
> We build the software your business actually runs on.
> Univarq designs, builds, and modernizes systems for companies that can't afford to get the architecture wrong. Senior engineers, not a bench of juniors learning on your codebase.

**Rewrite:**
> We build the software your business actually runs on.
>
> Univarq takes ownership of technology problems, from first sketch to production — frontend, backend, cloud, data, AI, whatever the problem calls for. We understand what you're trying to do, take it on as our own, and deliver it.

*Why: "first sketch to production" signals full-lifecycle ownership without leaning on the word architecture. The technology list shows range instead of specialization in one layer. The closing line reframes Univarq from "consultancy giving advice" to a team that understands the problem, owns it, and ships the outcome.*

---

## 2. About Univarq

**Rewrite:**
> Straight answers, not a committee.
>
> Univarq is a team that takes on hard technology problems and owns them, not a firm that hands you a recommendation and leaves the building. We've shipped systems at scale before — this isn't the first time.
>
> Frontend, backend, cloud infrastructure, data platforms, AI — we don't hand you off to a different specialist for every layer. The same team that understands the problem builds the solution, end to end.
>
> Some engagements, we sit inside your team and build. Others, we own the whole initiative. Either way, you're working directly with the people doing the work.

*Why: the third paragraph now does the job "architecture-first" used to do, but frames it as breadth-across-layers-without-handoffs rather than one specialty. "End to end" carries the completeness claim instead of repeating "architecture."*

## 3. What We Do — reframed around breadth, not four separate specialties

**Before** split things into Engineering / Architecture / AI & Automation / Technology Consulting, each with its own bullet list — which reads like four different teams rather than one team that covers the ground.

**Rewrite:**

> **Full-stack engineering, start to finish**
>
> One team, the whole stack. We build the frontend, the backend, the APIs that connect them, and the cloud infrastructure they run on — so you're not stitching together three vendors' worth of handoffs.
>
> - Web and enterprise applications, frontend to backend
> - Cloud infrastructure and platform engineering
> - System integration and API design
> - Data platforms and pipelines
> - AI-enabled features and workflow automation
> - Legacy modernization and technical strategy

*Why: instead of four parallel boxes that each sound like a different vendor, this presents it as one team with range — which is the actual pitch (a small senior team that doesn't need to sub out any layer of the work).*

## 4. Why Univarq

**Rewrite:**
> Why teams call Univarq
>
> **We own it, not just advise on it.** The people who understand your problem are the people building the fix.
>
> **One team, every layer.** Frontend, backend, cloud, data, AI — we don't outsource pieces to specialists you've never met.
>

*Why: "One team, every layer" and "We finish what we start" now carry the breadth-and-completeness message that used to route through "architecture first" and "engineering first."*
